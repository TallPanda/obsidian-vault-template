# Home Cooking — Fermentation

## Overview

Fermentation is the transformation of food by microorganisms — bacteria, yeast, or both. It is one of the oldest food preservation techniques and underlies bread, cheese, yoghurt, beer, wine, vinegar, kimchi, and miso.

## Types

**Lacto-fermentation** uses lactic acid bacteria naturally present on vegetables and in the environment. Salt suppresses harmful bacteria while allowing lactobacillus to thrive. Used for: sauerkraut, kimchi, pickles, yoghurt.

**Wild yeast fermentation** captures airborne yeast and bacteria in a flour-water mixture (a starter). Used for: sourdough bread, some flatbreads, traditional beer.

**Acetic acid fermentation** converts alcohol to acetic acid via *Acetobacter*. Used for: vinegar, kombucha.

## Getting Started

Sourdough is the most accessible entry point:
1. Mix equal weights of flour and water in a jar
2. Feed daily (discard half, add fresh flour and water)
3. After 5–7 days at room temperature you should see consistent bubbling and doubling

See the atomic note [[sourdough-fermentation-basics]] for more detail.

## Safety

Home fermentation is generally safe — the acid environment produced is hostile to pathogens. Key rules:
- Keep equipment clean but not sterile (sterile environments prevent the good organisms too)
- Trust your senses: off smells, unusual colours, or sliminess are reliable warning signs
- Never use iodised salt for lacto-fermentation — iodine kills the bacteria you're cultivating
